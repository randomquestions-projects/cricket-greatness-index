How the adjustments work

Era: Compare each batter against nearby-year peers, leaving the batter out of the reference baseline.

Opponent: Compare how other batters performed against that national side, with strong shrinkage. This is an opposition-level proxy, not individual historical bowler skill.

Home / away: Identify whether recorded venue is home, away or neutral; missing hosts remain unknown rather than imputed.

Match difficulty: Compare all other batters in the same match, removing the evaluated player's innings. This is a scoring proxy, not directly measured pitch quality.

Team carry: Value a 70 when teammates struggled more highly than an equivalent 70 in a high-scoring innings.

Peak and longevity: Show the maximum 25-innings quality window, career quality, and a JAWS-inspired index combining career contextual surplus and best seven nonconsecutive seasons.

JAWS inspiration, not baseball WAR

Baseball's JAWS combines career WAR and seven-year peak WAR. Cricket's provisional adaptation averages an observed career contextual-surplus measure and its seven best calendar-season surplus values, in the same units. Format-level percentiles are used for multi-format comparisons. Different numbers of matches per year create opportunity bias, so the index is explicitly experimental.

Historical fairness and omissions

All eras use the same batting scorecard fields for the core analysis. Missing historic delivery-level data, individual bowlers faced, detailed batting order, rule changes, exact ground conditions and series-level importance are not silently inferred. Match-level data rights for the older 2017 research snapshot are still unverified; these public dashboards show only derived aggregates.
